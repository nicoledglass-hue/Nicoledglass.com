Identity FAQ

FAQ.

What does culturally responsive therapy mean?

It means identity, race, culture, class, language, family history, belonging, and social realities can be considered as part of treatment rather than treated as outside the work.

Will identity become the only focus?

No. Identity can be clinically important without reducing a person to a category. Treatment can hold identity alongside desire, conflict, grief, shame, relationships, and self-understanding.

Do you offer culturally responsive therapy in Chicago?

Yes. Nicole Glass, MA, LCPC offers culturally responsive psychodynamic psychotherapy for adults in Chicago and throughout Illinois.

Identity-Informed Care

A reflective approach to what may be hard to name.

Treatment can attend to questions of identity, family history, race, culture, work, relationships, and self-worth without reducing a person's experience to a single category.

This approach supports adults who want to understand recurring patterns and the meanings that emerge through speaking, reflection, and sustained attention over time.

Culture and identity may shape how a person understands belonging, anger, desire, achievement, visibility, silence, loyalty, and separation. These questions can be clinically important without becoming the whole of a person.

Culturally responsive treatment allows social realities and private experience to be considered together. The work can hold family history, racial and cultural identity, class, language, professional life, and relationship patterns with care and depth.
