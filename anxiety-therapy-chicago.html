Anxiety Therapy in Chicago

When worry, tension, or dread becomes difficult to set down.

Anxiety may appear as overthinking, restlessness, irritability, difficulty sleeping, fear of disappointing others, or a sense of danger that does not have a clear source.

In psychodynamic and psychoanalytic therapy, anxiety is approached as something that has meaning. Treatment can help clarify what anxiety may be protecting against, what it may be trying to communicate, and how it is connected to relationships, history, desire, and conflict.

Treatment Focus

Anxiety can be listened to, not only managed.

Some people come to therapy after living for years with a mind that does not easily settle. Others notice anxiety most sharply in work, intimacy, family obligations, transitions, or the pressure to appear composed.

Therapy offers a consistent setting to speak about worry, shame, anger, longing, avoidance, perfectionism, and the ways a person may try to remain safe. Over time, patterns can become more visible and less automatic.

Anxiety may also be connected to questions of identity, professional pressure, family expectations, grief, attachment, or the fear of wanting too much. Rather than treating anxiety as a problem isolated from the rest of life, therapy can consider the conditions under which it intensifies and the meanings it carries.

What Can Be Explored

Anxiety may point toward conflict, longing, or protection.

In therapy, anxiety can be considered in relation to the specific forms it takes: worry that circles without resolution, pressure to anticipate what others need, tension before intimacy, fear of failure, dread before work, or the feeling of being watched and evaluated. These experiences may seem excessive, but they often carry a logic shaped by earlier relationships and present demands.

Psychoanalytic work can help distinguish what belongs to current reality from what is being repeated from another time. This distinction can make anxiety more thinkable, less isolating, and less likely to organize every decision.

The work may also attend to the body: tightness, vigilance, fatigue, or the sense of bracing for something unnamed. These signals can become part of what is listened to in treatment.

Anxiety FAQ

FAQ.

Can therapy help if I do not know why I am anxious?

Yes. Anxiety is often felt before it is understood. Treatment can help explore worry, tension, dread, avoidance, and the circumstances in which anxiety appears.

Is this only for panic attacks?

No. Anxiety therapy may also address overthinking, perfectionism, difficulty resting, fear of judgment, relationship anxiety, and persistent unease.
